# Findings from the CoS trial rounds — 3 October 2026

These were trial runs only. No deciding run was started. The verdict in every round below is therefore a go/no-go about whether the idea earned a real test.

## Round A — autocatalysis: make world structures easier to build

**Idea.** Let a nearby structure catalyse the next build, so structures could help make more structures. The design used named synthesis: the organism's genome named what to build, while available feedstock supplied the material.

**What was compared.** `FULL` (organisms build, with catalysis) was compared with `NOAC` (the same world without catalysis), `RAND` (random living organisms receive the building schedule), `RANDN` (the random version with named random pairs), `SHUF` (the builder trait is shuffled), and `W1` (the baseline world).

**Key numbers.** The diagnosis found 92.1 million BUILD executions; 84.4% were by non-builders. Of 9,161,885 founding attempts, only 50 founded a structure: 0.0005%, or 1 in 183,000. Catalysis made founding much more common, but in the v2 short trials FULL beat RAND on 2 of 3 seeds, RANDN on 1 of 3, and SHUF on 2 of 3. Structure income was only 0–4.5%. In the longer v2 check, FULL's late Lu was 0.40, 0.47, and 0.53 on seeds 93–95; RAND was 0.00, 1.73, and 0.00; RANDN was 0.00, 1.67, and 0.00; SHUF was 0.27, 2.67, and 1.07. FULL's trends were -0.007, -0.061, and 0.011.

**Verdict.** **NO-GO.** Structures were easier to create, but they did not reliably outperform the random and shuffled alternatives or earn a meaningful living.

**Commit.** `2757a7d`

## Round A2 — scarcity: make invention necessary for survival

**Idea.** Slowly reduce the payoff from the ordinary metabolism, leaving structures and channels untouched. If the easy food became less profitable, new structures might become worth building.

**What was compared.** `FULL` (catalysis plus scarcity) was compared with `NOAC`, `RAND`, `RANDN`, and `SHUF`; `FULLNS` was the same FULL design without scarcity. The chosen design was D1: metabolism fell to 0.25 over 100,000 ticks.

**Key numbers.** In the 450,000-tick check, seed 83 had FULL Lu 0.00 versus 0.27, 0.00, 0.00, and 0.27 for NOAC, RAND, RANDN, and SHUF; structure income was 0.0%. Seed 84 had FULL Lu 1.00 versus 0.07, 0.87, 2.87, and 0.20; its trend was -0.018 and structure income was 0.1%. Seed 85 had FULL Lu 0.20 versus 0.40, 1.13, 0.67, and 0.67; its trend was -0.025 and structure income was 0.0%. There were no reseeds in these final arms.

**Verdict.** **NO-GO.** Scarcity did not make world structures a useful source of income: they still earned 0.0–0.1%, and FULL failed the comparison and/or non-negative-trend requirements.

**Commit.** `b937cfe`

## Round A3 — heritable body chemistry

**Idea.** Move the building inside the organism. Each creature carries a set of catalysts, passes it to its child with copying errors, and can capture new catalysts. The chemistry is paid for by molecules already present in the creature's body.

**What was compared.** `FULL` inherited its parent's body. `NOINH` reset the body at birth. `RANDCAP` chose a captured catalyst's substrate at random instead of using what the parent had just made. `SHUF` copied a random living organism's body. `FIXED` restricted new and mutated catalysts to the base network's 1,024 reactions. `BASE` was the population reference.

**Key numbers.** In the selected B3 long check, FULL's Lu on seeds 73–75 was 85.53, 82.33, and 98.27, while its body supplied 31.9%, 26.9%, and 20.5% of all late income. RANDCAP's Lu was 70.53, 51.07, and 47.60. FULL's trends were -2.482, -1.407, and -1.039. All body arms survived without reseeding.

**Verdict.** **NO-GO.** The inherited chemistry clearly paid, but its useful novelty declined on every long-check seed, so the required non-negative trend was not met.

**Commit.** `33f208b`

## Round A3b — pathway fusion

**Idea.** The A3 diagnosis found that the one-step catalyst menu was running out: the available menu was 16,573, 16,446, and 16,643 reactions on seeds 73–75, and by 250,000 ticks the populations had tried 93–98% of it. Pathway fusion joined compatible catalysts into longer pathways, making the possible menu effectively inexhaustible and freeing body space.

**What was compared.** The same five body tests were used: inherited `FULL` versus `NOINH`, `RANDCAP`, `SHUF`, and `FIXED`, with `BASE` as the population reference. The selected long-check design was C1, fusion without the uncapped-body change.

**Key numbers.** On seeds 63–65, FULL's Lu was 31.00, 21.40, and 20.87. RANDCAP was 65.80, 35.00, and 31.73. FULL's body share of all late income was 38.9%, 40.8%, and 32.0%. FULL's trends were -4.746, 0.446, and -1.475.

**Verdict.** **NO-GO.** Random capture beat organism-directed capture on all three seeds, and FULL also had a negative trend on seeds 63 and 65.

**Commit.** `3b180b2`

## Round A4 — cross-feeding

**Idea.** Give each organism an internal chemical pool. Waste that its own catalysts cannot use leaks into the surrounding cell and can become food for neighbours, making the value of an invention depend on the surrounding community. The selected design, X2, also used scarcity.

**What was compared.** `FULL` leaked waste between organisms. `RANDCAP` used random capture. `NOLEAK` kept waste private except for release at death. `SHUFENV` leaked a random species instead of the actual waste. `NOINH` removed inheritance, and `FIXED` removed pathway fusion and kept only one-step base reactions. `BASE` was the population reference.

**Key numbers.** On seeds 53–55, FULL's Lu was 14.40, 20.07, and 21.33, versus RANDCAP's 53.93, 35.27, and 43.67. FULL's body share of all late income was 33.9%, 39.9%, and 44.4%. But cross-feeding supplied only 2.2%, 2.7%, and 4.1% of body income. FULL's trends were 0.461, -1.114, and 0.871. In the earlier smoke check, the share fell from 32% to 9%, while the no-leak comparison also reached 9% from death release alone.

**Verdict.** **NO-GO.** The ecosystem barely formed. RANDCAP beat FULL on every seed, and FULL also failed the trend requirement on seed 54.

**Commit.** `0535d6c`

## What we learned

- Structures placed in the world were never a living: even when catalysis and scarcity made them easier to build, they did not become a meaningful income source.
- Inherited body chemistry pays. Across the successful body trials, it supplied roughly 20–44% of all income.
- But organism-directed capture lost to random capture in A3b and A4. The creatures' history steered invention worse than chance.
- Cross-feeding barely formed: only 2–4% of body income in the A4 long check came from other organisms' leaked or released material.

## Candidate next directions

1. **Follow the capture result.** Treat random capture as the important comparison, and test why it beats capture guided by what the parent just made. The next design should preserve the body-income measurement while directly testing ways to stop organism-directed invention from exhausting or misreading its menu.
2. **Make cross-feeding a genuine ecological test.** The A4 result says the leak was too weak or too similar to death release to matter. A follow-up should make the distinction between neighbour leakage and death release explicit, while retaining `NOLEAK` and `SHUFENV` as nulls.
3. **Protect useful diversity rather than merely adding pathways.** A3b found no selective sweep and showed that fusion alone did not beat random capture. A candidate is to test whether keeping multiple inherited body lineages or preserving several successful pathways prevents convergence, with the same random-capture and shuffled-body nulls.
